Pre-Release · 2026

For most of my life, I have understood the world through patterns, logic, systems, and problems that could be broken down into smaller parts. That way of thinking has served me well. It has also become a place to hide.

These songs came from realizing that I had spent years becoming dependable, capable, productive, and useful while slowly losing contact with parts of myself that could not be measured on a dashboard or explained through a root-cause analysis.

Identity is not an album about technology, although technology is part of its language. The engineering metaphors are here because they are how I naturally make sense of the world. Protocols, rootkits, shortcuts, alerts, overrides, and broken processes become ways of talking about anxiety, relationships, exhaustion, communication, neurodivergence, responsibility, and the fear of becoming indistinguishable from the role you perform.

This album does not claim to answer those questions. It is an attempt to finally ask them honestly.

Tracklist

Presented in recommended listening order.

01

Executive Dysfunction

I can solve complex technical problems. I can lead engineers, design systems, navigate incidents, and find failures buried several layers deep. But capability is not the same thing as ease.

As my career progressed, I moved farther away from the direct engineering work I loved and deeper into strategy, organizational problems, meetings, politics, and decisions made far from the systems themselves. I became responsible for the machine instead of working inside it.

The song is frustrated, aggressive, and intentionally conflicted. It is proud of what I built. It is angry about what the work consumed. The central tension is simple: I built the machine. Now the machine owns me.

02

2AM Pager

The pager represents more than an on-call rotation. It represents a state of constant readiness: the belief that rest is temporary, peace is suspicious, and another emergency is always about to arrive.

You begin to measure your value by how quickly you respond. You become dependable to everyone except yourself. You learn how to wake up in the middle of the night and stabilize a system, while ignoring the quieter failures happening inside your own life.

The pager is also emotional — the internal alarm that never fully stops. When no emergency exists, do I still know who I am?

03

Shortcut

In engineering, a shortcut can be clever. In life, shortcuts are more complicated.

Sometimes productivity becomes a shortcut around vulnerability. Humor becomes a shortcut around grief. Logic becomes a shortcut around emotion. Work becomes a shortcut around conflict. Certainty becomes a shortcut around fear.

There are no instant fixes for identity, relationships, grief, or emotional connection. Some problems cannot be automated. Some wounds cannot be optimized away. The long way through may be the only honest way forward.

04

Rootkit

A rootkit is designed to hide deep inside a system. It gains privileged access, alters what the system can trust, and becomes difficult to detect from within. That made it the right metaphor for intrusive thoughts.

Anxiety does not always feel like an external voice. It can feel like your own reasoning. Once it has established itself, every signal can look suspicious. Is this thought mine? Is this fear evidence? Is my mind warning me, or has the warning system itself been compromised?

This song uses paranoia and body-horror imagery to express the experience of not being able to fully trust your internal signals.

05

Manual Override

The responsibilities are still being handled. The meetings are attended. The emails are answered. The problems are solved. From the outside, every diagnostic appears normal. But internally, something has stopped responding.

A manual override is necessary when the existing system cannot correct itself. In this song, that becomes a plea to interrupt the pattern before it becomes permanent. Continuing exactly as before is no longer an option.

At its heart, this is about wanting to feel again and not knowing how to begin.

06

Protocol

In networking, communication does not occur simply because a message was sent. The message must be received, understood, and acknowledged. Without that shared understanding, each side may believe it has communicated while the connection continues to fail. Relationships can work the same way.

One person may express love through actions: preparing something, remembering a need, leaving a light on. The other may express love through words, reassurance, attention, or the hope of being visibly chosen. Both people may be sincere. Both may be faithful. Both may still feel unseen.

This is not about the absence of love. It is about love that needs translation. We kept sending signals. Neither of us knew how to receive.

The Arc

These six songs were designed to be heard in order. Each track strips away another layer.

  1. Executive Dysfunction — This is what I became.
  2. 2AM Pager — This is what it cost.
  3. Shortcut — This is how I avoided it.
  4. Rootkit — This is what happened inside me.
  5. Manual Override — This is me asking for help.
  6. Protocol — This is me trying to reach you.

A Note on the Sound

Identity combines modern alternative rock, metalcore, rap rock, electronic textures, and moments of quieter vulnerability. The heavy sections represent overload, anger, pressure, and urgency. The rap-influenced sections provide space for dense ideas and internal monologue. The softer sections represent the moments after the alarms stop, when there is no volume left to hide behind.

Bass is especially important throughout. I do not only want music to be heard. I want it to be physically felt.

This is a pre-release version. These songs are being shared because I wanted them to exist outside of my own head before they were treated as finished products. Identity is about being seen more honestly, and that process cannot begin while everything remains hidden until it is perfect.
